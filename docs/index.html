28 EV-Normalized Analog & B&W Film Stocks

All 28 analog & B&W stocks are EV-normalized to Canon 6D 0 EV metering (Med EV -0.04..+0.01), rendered through the on-camera 12×17×17 YUV lattice (ship3) on 101 Canon 6D photos, and measured in CIEDE2000 against EV-matched Dehancer references.

What does ≤ 5.0 (CIEDE2000 ΔE00) mean?

ΔE00 is the ISO perceptual colour-difference scale. ≤ 2.0 is nearly indistinguishable to the naked eye; ≤ 5.0 is photographic print parity — the same film hue, contrast and mood without side-by-side instrumentation. Color-Only ΔE00 isolates hue/chroma accuracy at equal luminance, and P95 ΔE00 tracks the hardest 5% of pixels across all 101 test photos.

Honest Constraints

Known Limitations

Running real-time film grading inside a 2012 DSLR's ARM CPU and 8-bit YUV pipeline comes with real hardware trade-offs.

Hardware · Firmware

Canon EOS 6D (FW 1.1.6) Only

In-shutter grading hooks Canon's internal YUV encoder address (0xFF3442DC) specific to the 6D running firmware 1.1.6. Other Canon bodies or firmware versions are not supported.

Colour Precision

8-Bit YUV422 & 12×17×17 Lattice

Grades in-place on Canon's 8-bit YUV422 buffer rather than 32-bit desktop GPU floats. Subtle hue/saturation differences (ΔE00 ~ 2–4) remain visible in extreme blues, cyans, or deep shadows compared to desktop Dehancer.

Speed · Burst

Single-Shot Pace & CPU LiveView

LiveView grading runs on the ARM CPU (~12–20 fps depending on overlays), and in-shutter grading adds ~150–300 ms per frame — built for deliberate single shots rather than high-speed bursts. .CR2 RAW files stay untouched.

Spatial Effects

No Real-Time Optical Halation

In-shutter and LiveView passes focus on colour, tone curves, framing bars, and shot headers. Heavy spatial effects like physical halation bloom or full optical grain structure are limited compared to offline desktop plugins.
