film sim

Film colour for the Canon EOS 6D, running under Magic Lantern. Recipes, an in-camera engine, and a score for every stock.

A stock here is a recipe, not a filter: a tone curve, 48 per-hue sectors, a split tone for shadows and highlights, halation and grain. The same recipe renders two ways — a full-precision RGB develop, and a 12×17×17 lattice baked for the camera's YUV buffer — so the picture on the screen and the file on the card are the same look.

The lattice

LiveView and the in-shutter grade both run against a baked table with 12 nodes along luma and 17 along each chroma axis — SE_YUV3_NY and SE_YUV3_NC, 3468 nodes. It is rebuilt when the stock changes, not per frame.

How a stock is scored

Each recipe renders every frame of a 101-frame corpus through the same engine, is compared against a Dehancer reference of that frame in CIEDE2000 per pixel, and scored by the median per frame. Twenty stocks carry a measured score. Lower is better.

Colour research

The interesting questions turned out to be measurable ones:

  • Classic Negative is Fujicolor Superia. Against the Superia 200 reference, SUP200.fs scores a median ΔE00 of 3.07 on frame 001, while the hand-written classicneg.fs scores 9.43 — three times the error — which is why it is kept out of calibrated comparisons.
  • Ektar 100 and Portra 160 look alike, and the reference says the same. Our renders separate them by a median 5.19 ΔE00; Dehancer's own renders of the two separate them by only 3.74. The likeness is in the films.
  • Grain was invisible because the amplitude floored to zero. grain × 62 / 100 × 2 / 7 returned 0 for every stock with GRAIN ≤ 6, so Ektar and Portra drew no grain at all. The curve is now 4/7 with a floor of 2.
What this page does not claim. Scores are against Dehancer, not against film. A corpus of 101 frames is a sample, not a law. The three-up strip in the repository shows that recipes differ, not that they are right — the number is the evidence.
