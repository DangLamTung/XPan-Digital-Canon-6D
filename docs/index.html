How a stock is scored

Each recipe renders every frame of a 101-frame corpus through the same engine, is compared against a Dehancer reference of that frame in CIEDE2000 per pixel, and scored by the median per frame. Twenty of the 46 shipped recipes have a score; the rest are hand-written looks, not calibrated stocks.

The lattice that runs on the camera has 12 nodes along luma and 17 along each chroma axis — SE_YUV3_NY and SE_YUV3_NC, 3468 nodes — rebuilt when the stock changes, not per frame.

Why a lattice, and how it is cut

A frame is graded in the camera, between Canon writing the YUV buffer and Canon encoding it. Running the full RGB engine there is far too slow, so the recipe is baked into a three-dimensional table over luma and the two chroma axes, and the pixels are graded by interpolating in it.

Equal steps on Cb and Cr waste nodes on colours no scene contains: an 8 × 8 × 8 cube costs 11.4 levels of mean error on a real frame. Widening only the chroma axes, to 17, brings that to 7.0 at the same 12 nodes along luma — which is why the table is 12 × 17 × 17 and not a cube.

Luma keeps its own 256-entry curve; each of the 3468 nodes carries three signed bytes — how much brighter or darker that colour grades than grey of the same luma (dy), and where its chroma goes. Dropping dy fixes chroma but leaves luma 3.4× worse, because a saturated pixel does not grade to the same brightness as a grey one.

The table is cut once per stock change, not per frame: the engine is sampled at the node positions, the result becomes ML/DATA-side state in RAM, and every frame afterwards is three interpolations per pixel.

Where it hooks on the 6D

fs_yuvhook.c patches sdfExecuteYuvToJpegPath at 0xFF3442DC — the still-JPEG path — so the grade lands on the file the camera writes. LiveView is graded through the same tables, which is why the preview and the shot agree. Canon is not modified on disk: the hook is installed at module load and removed with it.

The 65:24 framing is a separate matter: the camera still writes 3:2, and the bars are burned in afterwards. Canon's own encoder crop is not used — the code that would control it sits outside the 16 MB ROM image available for the 6D, which is documented in docs/xpan-crop-port.md.

Colour research

  • Classic Negative is Fujicolor Superia. Against the Superia 200 reference SUP200.fs scores a median ΔE00 of 3.07 on frame 001, while the hand-written classicneg.fs scores 9.43 — three times the error, which is why it stays out of calibrated comparisons.
  • Ektar 100 and Portra 160 look alike, and the reference agrees. Our renders separate them by 5.19 ΔE00; Dehancer's own renders separate the two films by only 3.74. The likeness is in the films, not in the code.
  • Grain was invisible because the amplitude floored to zero. grain × 62 / 100 × 2 / 7 returned 0 for every stock with GRAIN ≤ 6, so Ektar and Portra drew none. The curve is now 4/7 with a floor of 2.
What this page does not claim. Scores are against Dehancer, not against film. A corpus of 101 frames is a sample, not a law. The numbered table is the evidence; the pictures are only illustration.
