A stock here is a recipe, not a filter: a tone curve, 48 per-hue sectors, a split tone, halation and grain. It renders twice — a full-precision RGB develop and a 12 × 17 × 17 lattice for the camera's YUV buffer — so the picture on the screen and the file on the card are the same look.

How a stock is scored

Each recipe renders every frame of a 101-frame corpus through the same engine, is compared against a Dehancer reference of that frame in CIEDE2000 per pixel, and scored by the median per frame. Twenty of the 46 shipped recipes have a score; the rest are hand-written looks, not calibrated stocks.

The lattice that runs on the camera has 12 nodes along luma and 17 along each chroma axis — SE_YUV3_NY and SE_YUV3_NC, 3468 nodes — rebuilt when the stock changes, not per frame.

Colour research

  • Classic Negative is Fujicolor Superia. Against the Superia 200 reference SUP200.fs scores a median ΔE00 of 3.07 on frame 001, while the hand-written classicneg.fs scores 9.43 — three times the error, which is why it stays out of calibrated comparisons.
  • Ektar 100 and Portra 160 look alike, and the reference agrees. Our renders separate them by 5.19 ΔE00; Dehancer's own renders separate the two films by only 3.74. The likeness is in the films, not in the code.
  • Grain was invisible because the amplitude floored to zero. grain × 62 / 100 × 2 / 7 returned 0 for every stock with GRAIN ≤ 6, so Ektar and Portra drew none. The curve is now 4/7 with a floor of 2.
What this page does not claim. Scores are against Dehancer, not against film. A corpus of 101 frames is a sample, not a law. The numbered table is the evidence; the pictures are only illustration.
